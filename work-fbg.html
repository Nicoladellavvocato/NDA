PRJ.02

Embedded FBG Sensors for SHM of CFRP Sandwich Structures

BSc thesis · Politecnico di Torino · June 2026 · Embedded sensing & modal testing

FBGCFRP sandwichAbaqus/CAEImpact hammerMATLABFFT

My bachelor's thesis investigates Fibre Bragg Grating (FBG) optical sensors embedded inside carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) sandwich structures for structural health monitoring (SHM) — combining a finite element model of the panel with an experimental modal test campaign.

01 Why SHM, and why FBG

Composite structures are central to modern aerospace because of their strength-to-weight ratio, but their damage modes — matrix cracking, delamination, fibre breakage — can develop internally and are not always visible from the surface. Structural health monitoring aims to instrument a structure so its condition can be assessed continuously, rather than only at scheduled inspections.

Fibre Bragg gratings are well suited to this role. A grating inscribed in an optical fibre reflects a narrow wavelength band centred on a Bragg wavelength; when the fibre is strained or its temperature changes, that reflected wavelength shifts. Reading the shift turns the fibre into a precise strain (and temperature) sensor.

  • Lightweight & small — minimal mass and intrusion, important for composite parts.
  • Immune to EMI — optical, so unaffected by electromagnetic interference.
  • Multiplexable — many gratings along a single fibre, each at its own wavelength, giving distributed sensing.
  • Embeddable — fibres can be bonded to the surface or integrated between plies of the laminate.

02 Specimen & embedded sensor

The work centres on a CFRP sandwich sample in which a Fibre Bragg Grating optical fibre was embedded directly between the carbon plies during lay-up, so that the grating experiences the strain field inside the laminate. An optical interrogator illuminates the fibre and tracks the reflected Bragg wavelength over time, providing the raw signal from which strain is derived.

Sensing principle

A shift in the reflected Bragg wavelength maps to mechanical strain through the grating's gauge factor — turning the embedded fibre into a strain sensor that sits inside the structure rather than on its surface.

03 Finite element model

Before testing, I built a finite element model of the panel in Abaqus/CAE to predict its natural frequencies and strain mode shapes. The model tells where the strain of each mode is concentrated and what the embedded grating should see when the panel vibrates.

04 Impact-hammer test campaign

The instrumented panel was excited with an impact hammer while the interrogator recorded the dynamic strain measured by the embedded FBG — an experimental modal test performed with an optical sensor instead of conventional accelerometers.

05 Signal processing & comparison

The recorded strain signals were processed in MATLAB:

  • Wavelength → strain — converting the measured Bragg-wavelength shift into strain.
  • FFT & spectral analysis — moving the time histories into the frequency domain to identify the panel's resonant peaks.
  • Model comparison — relating the measured frequencies and strain response to the Abaqus predictions.
Note

Detailed quantitative results, specimen specifications and the full experimental protocol are reported in the thesis document. The summary here is intentionally qualitative.

06 Conclusions & future work

The thesis explores embedded FBG sensing as a way to monitor composite sandwich structures from the inside, combining the low mass and electromagnetic immunity of optical fibres with the ability to read the dynamic behaviour of the panel. Natural extensions include networks of multiplexed gratings for wider spatial coverage and vibration-based damage detection on real aerospace structures.
